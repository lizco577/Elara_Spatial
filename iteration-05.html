How it works

Five steps from photo to estimate.

The process

Purpose-built to slot into the workflow you already run

The output is audit-ready damage documentation your adjusters review, edit and approve. The intelligence is AI. The judgment stays yours.

01

Capture

A policyholder, adjuster or contractor takes photos of the damaged space with a standard smartphone — no app download needed. The flow runs in a mobile browser and walks the user through every angle. Over 90% of first-time users complete the capture successfully on their first try.

02

Privacy filter

Before anything else, an automated filter removes personally identifiable information — faces, family photos, documents, screens, license plates. Everyone downstream sees only anonymised data, and the originals never travel further.

03

3D reconstruction

A generative model rebuilds the room in three dimensions from the anonymised photos. Adjusters can read layout, dimensions and surfaces — often without ever needing to be on site.

04

Damage and material identification

Computer vision identifies rooms, objects, materials and visible damage, then generates a complete floor plan with everything labelled and measured.

05

Rules-based estimate

A traditional, rules-based engine applies carrier guidelines, rates and coverage rules to produce a detailed line-item estimate. The estimate, floor plan and supporting documentation are QA'd by a human before delivery.

Deployment

One platform, four ways to deploy

Estimate quality stays consistent regardless of who is holding the phone — so you can reserve experienced adjusters for QA and complex claims.

01.Policyholder at FNOL

Capture happens during the first notice of loss call. They receive a link, walk the rooms and take photos.

02.Mitigation technician

Before or after work, the technician documents scope, pre-existing conditions and the full extent of damage.

03.Field adjuster

Documentation onsite in minutes instead of hours. A full estimate proposal is waiting back at the desk.

04.Desk adjuster

The adjuster calls the policyholder, asks for the capture, and reviews the paperwork without leaving their desk.

Our story

The property world has lived with this problem for decades. There's a gap between what is physically there and the damage that can actually be seen, measured, documented and acted on.

Elara Spatial was founded by an architect trained in computer vision at MIT, alongside a team of technologists from Silicon Valley and the construction industry, specifically to close it. The insurance industry wastes billions a year in inspection time, inconsistent estimates and disputes that drag on long after a homeowner needs an answer — and adjusters risk losing their time, energy and, far too often, policyholders' trust.

We set our sights on interior property damage, where the largest information gaps are. Our name reflects the work: Elara, one of Jupiter's many moons, is a quiet satellite orbiting something large and complex. Our first exploration is what the claims process looks like when spatial intelligence is embedded in it.
