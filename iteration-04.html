How it works

Five steps from photo to estimate.

Purpose-built to slot into the workflow your team already runs. Here's exactly what happens between the first photo and the finished estimate.

The process

The output is audit-ready damage documentation that your adjusters review, edit and approve. The intelligence is AI. The judgment is yours.

01

Capture

A policyholder, adjuster or contractor takes photos of the damaged space with a standard smartphone — no app download needed. The flow runs in a mobile browser and walks the user through every angle. Over 90% of first-time users complete the capture successfully on their first try.

02

Privacy filter

Before anything else, an automated privacy filter removes personally identifiable information — faces, family photos, documents, screens, license plates. Everyone working with the images sees only anonymised data.

03

3D reconstruction

A generative model rebuilds the room in 3D from the anonymised photos. Adjusters can understand layout, dimensions and surfaces — often without ever needing to be on site.

04

Damage and material identification

Computer vision identifies rooms, objects, materials and visible damage. The system generates a complete floor plan with everything labelled and measured.

05

Rules-based estimate

A traditional, rules-based estimation engine applies carrier guidelines, rates and coverage rules to produce a detailed line-item estimate. The estimate, floor plan and supporting documentation are then QA'd by a human before delivery.

Company

It all started with a simple question: why is it still so hard to know what's really inside a building?

The property world has lived with this problem for decades. There's a gap between what's physically there and the damage that can actually be seen, measured, documented, and acted on. Elara Spatial was founded by an architect trained in computer vision at MIT and a team of technologists from Silicon Valley and the construction industry, specifically to solve it.

The insurance industry wastes billions of dollars a year in inspection time, inconsistent estimates, and disputes that drag on long after a homeowner needs an answer. Adjusters risk losing their time, energy and — far too often — policyholders' trust. We've set our sights on interior property damage, where the largest information gaps are.
