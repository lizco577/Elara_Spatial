Types of Assessments

One platform. Four ways to deploy.

[Product Name] adapts to your existing workflow. Today, we support four primary capture scenarios:

Policyholder at FNOL

Capture happens during the first notice of loss (FNOL) call. The policyholder gets a link, walks through the rooms, and takes photos.

Mitigation Technician

Before or after work is done, the technician can capture photos to document the scope, note pre-existing conditions, and show the full extent of damage.

Field Adjuster

Documentation happens onsite in minutes instead of hours. Once back at the office, the adjuster has a full estimate proposal, with all relevant documentation, waiting for review.

Desk Adjuster

After a claim is opened at FNOL, the adjuster can call the policyholder and ask them to do the capture — reviewing the finished paperwork without ever leaving their desk.

Anyone can capture a claim without compromising estimate quality. That means you can reserve your most experienced adjusters for QA and complex claims.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does this only work for water claims today?Human expertise is hard to replace with AI, so we broke the problem into manageable pieces. Water damage is the most common peril in property insurance, so that's where we started. There's more to come on our roadmap.

Does the solution only work for small claims?No. [Product Name] handles claims of any size. But policyholders may not want to document large claims themselves. The sweet spot for policyholder-driven captures is usually 2–3 rooms per claim. Anything larger is usually captured by adjusters, inspectors, or contractors.

Can the solution be used in the field?Yes. At least one carrier uses it that way successfully. However, every estimate we create is QA'd by a human, so we're not yet able to settle claims onsite. It may be possible in the future, though.

Does the solution work after mitigation?Yes. [Product Name] can produce estimates both before and after mitigation.

Can [Product Name] make coverage decisions?No. That's by design. We don't have access to coverage information, and don't believe AI should be making coverage decisions. Regulators agree. [Product Name] only documents what's there. Humans still decide what's covered.

What happens during a catastrophe?We support customers through CAT events and extreme weather. Normally, we don't process claims on weekends or holidays. We do, however, work extended hours every day of the week during a crisis.

Why is it still so hard to know what's really inside a building?
